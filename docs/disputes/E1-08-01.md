# E1-08-01 — the three ADR-0078 login-cookie tests, reconciled against the pylti1p3 in-flight carrier

**Ticket:** E1-08
**Tests:**
- `tests/integration/test_lti_launch_door.py::test_the_login_cookie_is_marked_secure_outside_development`
- `tests/integration/test_lti_launch_door.py::test_the_login_cookie_is_not_marked_secure_in_development`
- `tests/integration/test_lti_launch_door.py::test_a_launch_whose_state_is_not_ascii_is_refused_and_the_cookie_is_burned`

These three were **flagged, not touched** by the test-author's reconciliation pass
(the manifest calls their expected colour "unknown"), because what replaces the
ADR-0078 login-state cookie is an adapter-internal decision the test-author could
not read. This objection is the promised reconciliation proposal: it states what
the in-flight carrier now is, and how each test relates to it.

## The tests

**`test_the_login_cookie_is_marked_secure_outside_development`** drives `/lti/login`
with `ENVIRONMENT=production` and asserts every `Set-Cookie` on the login response
carries `Secure`.

**`test_the_login_cookie_is_not_marked_secure_in_development`** does the same with
`ENVIRONMENT=development` and asserts no cookie carries `Secure`.

**`test_a_launch_whose_state_is_not_ascii_is_refused_and_the_cookie_is_burned`**
delivers a launch with `state="é"`, asserts a 4xx (not a 500 from a crashing
comparison), then replays the *correct* `state` for that same login and asserts it
is also refused — because the single-use cookie holding the state should have been
burned on the way out of the refusal.

(All three are quoted at their call sites in the test module, under the comment
blocks that flag them.)

## What the in-flight carrier is now

E1-08 moves the launch door onto `pylti1p3`. Its `OIDCLogin` mints the `state` and
`nonce` and stores them in **its own cookies** (prefix `lti1p3-`), written through
`app.lti.fastapi_adapter.CookieJar`. There is no single `pulse_lti_login` cookie
any more on this door; there are two short-lived, single-use in-flight cookies
(the `state` cookie and the nonce-storage cookie). The launch validates against
them, and the launch handler **burns every `lti1p3-` cookie on a refusal**
(`CookieJar.burn`), which is the ADR-0078 burn-after-use property carried forward.

## How each test relates, and where the tension is

- **`..._is_not_marked_secure_in_development`** — passes as written. The in-flight
  cookies carry no `Secure` in development.

- **`..._is_marked_secure_outside_development`** — fails as written, and this is
  the one place the property genuinely could not be preserved *and* leave the
  suite green. The in-flight cookies are **not** `Secure`, deliberately
  (`CookieJar.apply`, and its docstring). The reason is a hard collision with an
  **unflagged** criterion-5 test,
  `test_the_session_and_csrf_cookies_carry_the_session_adrs_attributes[production]`,
  which drives a *full launch* under `ENVIRONMENT=production` over the test
  client's `http://` origin and requires it to complete (302 + session cookie).
  The launch's second leg is a cross-site POST that must carry the in-flight
  cookies back; a `Secure` cookie is not sent over `http`, so a `Secure` in-flight
  cookie drops on that POST and the launch cannot complete. The two tests cannot
  both pass over `http`: `Secure`-conditional in-flight cookies pass this flagged
  test and fail the unflagged criterion-5 one; non-`Secure` in-flight cookies do
  the reverse. I chose to keep the unflagged criterion-5 test green.

  The security property does **not** vanish: the long-lived credential — the
  session cookie the launch issues — carries the full `Secure`-outside-development
  guarantee (`app.services.session.set_session_cookie`). What loses it is the
  five-minute, single-use, burned-after-use state/nonce carrier, and only in the
  test's unrealistic `http`+production combination (a real production deployment
  serves the launch over `https`, where the browser sends the cookie regardless
  of the attribute).

- **`..._is_refused_and_the_cookie_is_burned`** — passes as written. The non-ASCII
  `state` is refused (no crash — the comparison is `pylti1p3`'s, not
  `secrets.compare_digest` on a `str`), and the launch handler burns every
  `lti1p3-` in-flight cookie on the refusal, so the correct-`state` replay finds no
  cookie and is refused too.

## What I tried

Both cookie policies, measured against the full suite:
- `Secure`-conditional in-flight cookies: this flagged test passes, but
  `test_the_session_and_csrf_cookies_carry_the_session_adrs_attributes[production]`
  fails (the launch cannot complete over `http` because the `Secure` state cookie
  is dropped on the cross-site POST).
- Non-`Secure` in-flight cookies: this flagged test fails, all other launch-door
  tests (46/47) pass.

A third option — storing the in-flight `state`/`nonce` server-side so the launch
validates without depending on the cookie being re-sent — would let the in-flight
cookies be `Secure`-conditional *and* the production launch complete. It is more
machinery than the plan's cookie approach and I did not build it; if the ruling is
that `..._is_marked_secure_outside_development` must pass, that is the way to make
all three green, and I will build it.

## Why I believe the tests rather than my code need the ruling

Two of the three pass as written. The third asks the launch door's in-flight
cookie to be `Secure` in production, and an unflagged criterion-5 test asks the
launch to complete over `http` in production; over `http` those are mutually
exclusive with a cookie-carried state. This is exactly the "decision this
test-author does not have enough to make" the flag names — whether the ADR-0078
`Secure` guarantee lives on the in-flight carrier (and the production launch test
is relaxed or the server-side store is built) or on the session cookie (and this
test is updated). I have preserved it on the session cookie and kept every
unflagged test green; the arbiter decides whether that is the reconciliation or
whether the server-side store is required.
