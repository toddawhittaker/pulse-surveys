## What I believe it asserts incorrectly

It expects `mock-lms-user-biol-215-r3ww-student-07` to hold posted scores at
2026-10-05 and 2026-10-12. He holds none, at any moment of the drive, because
**Pulse never enrolled him**: the platform reported him `Inactive` at the first
sync, and a member who is already dropped when the tool first sees them gets no
`enrollment` row.

Measured on the driven stack, after the sweeps at every clock position:

## The spec text I am relying on

SPEC §3.4's last line is the whole of what the specification says about drops:

> - Re-posted whenever a recomputation changes the value, ordinarily after each
>   week closes; fully automatic, no instructor action or override. …
> - Late adds and missed weeks select **which weeks' items form the denominator**,
>   not a count of weeks. The denominator starts at the student's first enrolled
>   week (from NRPS enrollment data). …
> - Drops: scores stop updating; the LMS owns what happens to the column.

The spec settles what happens to a student who *was* enrolled and leaves. It says
nothing about a member the platform already reports as departed the first time
the tool reads the roster, and that gap is where E1 made a decision:

`docs/adr/0095-the-roster-syncs-enrollment-windows-and-what-it-refuses.md`,
under alternatives rejected:

> **Record a closed enrollment for a member who is already dropped on the first
> sync.** Attractive — it would keep some history for a student who left before
> the tool arrived — and it is unwritable: `started_on` would have to be
> synthesized, and ADR 0023's `ended_on >= started_on` refuses a row whose end
> date precedes the sync that first saw it. So such a member gets no row, which
> is the honest answer to "how many weeks was this student enrolled in, as far as
> Pulse knows".

The code says the same thing in one branch
(`backend/app/services/roster_sync.py::_record_enrollment`):

```python
    if member.dropped:
        if open_row is not None:
            ended = member.window_end.date() if member.window_end is not None else today
            _close(session, open_row, ended_on=ended, window_end=member.window_end)
        return
```

## What I tried

Nothing, deliberately. Two things could make these assertions pass and I believe
both are wrong:

1. **Enroll a member the platform reports as `Inactive`.** That reverses ADR
   0095's argued rejection, changes E1's roster ingestion from inside E3's exit
   ticket, and would post a grade for a student the LMS says is not currently a
   member of the section. It also has the problem ADR 0095 names: `started_on`
   for such a member is a date nothing supplies.
2. **Read the extension's `end` in preference to `status`.** The mock seeds this
   member with `status="Inactive"` *and* `closed_at` 2026-10-19 precisely because
   the two can disagree (`mock-lms/app/seed.py`: "a seed where `status` and `end`
   disagree is one E1 has to pick a side in"). E1 picked `status`. Picking the
   other side in E3-08 would be a silent reversal of that, and it is not a change
   any test in this ticket asks for.

The rest of the drive was verified against the implementation rather than left
unknown. Every other row of the exit table is produced correctly, including the
half of row 7 that this objection does **not** touch:

- the learner's MATH drop (P4): at the 2026-10-05 sweep every other MATH member
  re-posts with a six-week ledger and the learner does not; the value standing
  for him is the 16.0 posted at P3 — exit row 7's "scores stop updating and the
  last posted value stands", proven with a positive control in the same sweep;
- exit rows 1–6, all green in the run (`P1`, `P2`, `P3` passed; `P5`'s NURS
  assertions passed; `P4`'s failure was objection E3-08-01's, later in the case
  than the row-4, row-5 and row-6 subjects);
- P6's arithmetic, driven by hand at 2026-10-26 after the case could not run:
  the learner reads `28.6` with a seven-line ledger, a day-one classmate `0.0`
  with seven lines, the platform-dated late add `0.0` with a ledger starting at
  week 4 — the hand-computed values the spec carries, to the digit.

## Why I believe the test rather than my code is at fault

The prediction the three assertions rest on is falsifiable, the run falsified it,
and the file says in as many words that the run is what settles it. The behaviour
it met is not an accident of this implementation: it is a decision with an ADR, a
rejected alternative, and a reason that still holds — there is no honest
`started_on` for a student who left before the tool ever saw the roster.

**What I suggest, for the arbitrator and the test author.** Exit-table row 7 with
its own seeded member is still drivable, and it is the work order's original
prediction: **07 receives no score at any position of the drive**, asserted at
P4, P5 and P6 with his classmates' posts in the same sweeps as the positive
control that makes the absence mean something. That is a real case — a member the
platform reports as gone is never given a gradebook column at all — and it pairs
with the learner's MATH drop, which is the other half: a member who *was*
enrolled stops being updated and keeps his last value. P6 then loses its premise
guard and becomes a third position at which the absence holds while two
classmates gain a seven-line entry.

**If instead the arbitrator reads "Inactive with an end date three weeks out" as
a student who has not yet left**, this is not an implementation decision: it
reverses ADR 0095 and changes what E1 ingests, which is outcome 3 in
`docs/disputes/README.md` — Todd's call, and a spec sentence or a superseding ADR
rather than a number in a test or a branch in E3-08.
