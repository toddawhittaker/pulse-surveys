## What I believe it asserts incorrectly

It treats "is an entry in the backend copy registry" as "is read by a student".
Those were the same thing while the registry held only the survey's words; they
are not the same thing now. The sweep has no notion of a surface or an audience,
so it cannot distinguish a bounce a student reads from a 403 only a leadership
session can provoke.

E5-13 is required by three other tests in this branch's tests commit to publish
the named-set API's eight refusal sentences as registry entries under
`leadership_comparison_sets.`:

- `tests/unit/test_the_shipped_copy_inventory_holds_to_items_four_and_five.py::test_the_collector_finds_the_two_refusals_the_named_set_api_answers`
- `...::test_the_comparison_set_refusal_constants_say_what_the_registry_says`
- `...::test_the_comparison_set_surface_owes_no_line_and_collects_from_both_its_sources`

Seven of those eight sentences contain the words "comparison set", because a
comparison set is the thing being refused. With the entries published, the sweep
above reports all seven as strings that "show a student a comparison". The two
requirements cannot both be met by any implementation: one test requires the
entries to exist with exactly those texts, the other forbids those texts in the
registry at all.

## The spec text I am relying on

SPEC §4.1, with the sentence before and the items either side:

> ### 4.1 Hard visibility invariants (testable)
>
>  1. Students never see comparables, benchmarks, university averages, or other
>     sections — in charts, text, tooltips, exports, or aria labels. *(Asserted
>     from **E2**, the first epic with a student-visible path and the scoping
>     that gives "another section" its meaning.)*

The subject of item 1 is the student: what a *student* sees. The named-set
refusals are answered only to a request that reaches
`app.api.leadership`, and the first of the eight,
`leadership_comparison_sets.not_leadership`, is exactly what anybody who is not
a leadership session is answered with instead of the screen. No student can
reach any of the other seven: they are refusals about a set being created,
edited, deleted or read, all behind `require_leadership` /
`csrf_verified_leadership`.

SPEC §4.1 item 4, which is the item that does govern this vocabulary on a
leadership surface, is the one the inventory asserts over these strings:
aggregate language counts sections and never instructors, "needs attention"
never "underperforming", no ranking and no composite scores. Saying "comparison
set" breaks none of it.

## What I tried

1. Publishing the eight as `CopyEntry` values with their texts unchanged, which
   is what the three tests above require and what the E5-13 work order settles.
   The three go green and this sweep goes red on seven of the eight.
2. Looking for an exemption the sweep already offers — a surface map, a key
   list, an audience field on `CopyEntry`. There is none: `every_entry()` is
   every module's `COPY` merged, and `CopyEntry` is pinned to exactly two fields
   (`key`, `text`) by
   `test_the_copy_package_publishes_a_frozen_copy_entry_of_a_key_and_a_text`, so
   an audience marker on the entry is refused too.
3. Considering rewording the eight sentences to avoid the phrase. Rejected: the
   texts are pinned unchanged by the E5-13 ruling, the sentences have to name
   the rule that was broken to be any use to the leader reading them, and
   `tests/unit/test_the_placeholder_era_prose_is_swept_from_the_payload_path.py`
   quotes one of them as a sample its own sweep must leave alone. Rewording to
   dodge a word list would also be the "quiet reword" the inventory's own
   failure messages forbid.

## Why I believe the test rather than my code is at fault

The same words already ship to the same reader, on the same screen, through the
other half of the same surface, and nothing calls that a violation. The
comparison-set screen's own copy module,
`frontend/src/copy/leadershipComparisonSetCopy.ts`, says "comparison set" five
times, and `frontend/src/copy/instructorReportStatCopy.ts` publishes three keys
spelled `instructor_report_stats.benchmark_withheld*`. This sweep reads the
backend registry only, so it has never seen either. If the words were genuinely
a §4.1 item 1 violation wherever they appear, the frontend copy directory would
be the larger violation and the epic would be unshippable. What the sweep is
really measuring is which side of the wire a string is written on, which is not
what item 1 is about.

The inventory module next door has already made the audience distinction
explicit for this surface: `GOVERNED_SURFACES` claims the
`leadership_comparison_sets.` prefix, and the surface is recorded in
`SURFACES_WITH_NO_CONFIDENTIALITY_LINE` because nothing on it is anybody's
response. The registry sweep is the one place that still assumes a single
audience for every string in the package.

The repair I would suggest, for the arbitrator rather than as an instruction: the
sweep keeps its vocabulary and gains a reader's audience — sweep the entries
whose surface is student-facing, with the leadership and instructor surfaces
named and argued in the test the way the inventory names its own exemptions, so
that the emptiness trap (`docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 3) is still closed by the
student surfaces' entries continuing to be swept. Any repair is the test
author's to write; I have changed nothing under `tests/`.

## Ruling (2026-09-15)

Ruled on sources, both ways. Neither side was wholly right.

**The implementer's premise is wrong in one place.** "No student can reach any of
the eight" is false for the first: `app.api.deps.require_leadership` answers the
`not_leadership` sentence to any session that is not a leadership session, a
student's included, and its text begins "Comparison sets are defined by
leadership". SPEC §4.1 item 1 says students never see comparables "in text", and
E2's ruling made the word list the currency that sentence is checked in. So that
sentence, as written, is exactly what the sweep exists to refuse, and the sweep
was right to red on it. The other seven are answered only behind
`require_leadership` (the 404, the 403, the 409, and the four 422s of
`app.api.leadership` and `app.services.comparison_sets`), and a student cannot
be served any of them.

**The test's premise is wrong in the other place.** Its docstring says the rule
"is about what a student reads", and its mechanism — every entry in the
registry — was a faithful proxy for that while every registry string was a
student's. The registry now holds sentences only a leader can be served, and
the inventory module next door already models that audience split by surface.
A sweep with no audience model is the closed-set guard `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry
53 describes, one level out: what encloses the set is who can be served the
string.

**Decisions.**

1. `leadership_comparison_sets.not_leadership` is reworded so that it names no
   comparison set: "This request does not carry a leadership session." The
   `deps.py` comment already argues for one sentence covering absent, malformed,
   expired, foreign and wrong-role sessions, and that argument does not need the
   words "comparison sets" to hold. The work order's "texts unchanged" was a
   ruling of this ticket, and the spec outranks it. The implementer makes this
   change.
2. The sweep gains an exemption **by key, not by surface**, in the shape ADR
   0158's small-N exemption set: a named tuple of the seven registry keys that
   are answered only behind `require_leadership`, each with the gate and the
   route that guards it written beside it, and the sweep skips exactly those
   keys. By key rather than by surface because the surface has one key a
   student can be served, and that key stays inside the sweep. Controls, in the
   host module's own shape: a synthetic entry carrying "benchmark" under a
   student prefix is still caught; the `not_leadership` key is asserted present
   in `every_entry()` and not in the exempt tuple; and removing an exemption row
   puts its key back in the count (the inventory module's
   `test_removing_an_exemption_row_puts_its_key_back_in_the_count` is the
   precedent). The test author writes this.
3. The narrowing is a change to what a §4.1 gate covers and is recorded as
   such: ADR 0177 (context, decision, alternatives — sweep by surface; reword
   all eight; an audience field on `CopyEntry`, which its two-field pin
   refuses — and consequences, including that a leadership-only refusal that
   later becomes reachable by a student must leave the tuple in the same
   change). The pull request body names the coverage given up: seven sentences
   no student can be served.

Dispute closed by the ruling above once both changes are in the tree and the
module is green.
