# E5-12 — attempts

## 2026-09-13 — the tests, written from the criteria

Six files: a fixture-less helper module (`tests/fixtures/benchmark_history.py`,
indexed in `tests/conftest.py`'s last group) and five test modules — the two guard
refusals, the foreign-row refusal, the two source sweeps over the seeder's dates,
the three calendar tests over the prior term's own start-letter map, and the two
self-check counting tests. `docs/MISTAKES.md` was not edited and no counter was
bumped. What the writing turned on:

- **A student cannot hold two responses in one week of one section.** The work
  order describes the self-check plant as "a student with two responses in one
  week"; E2-05's `uq_response_user_id_section_id_week_id` refuses that row. The
  shape that actually makes a distinct-student count diverge from a response count
  is one student enrolled in **two sections of the same cohort** answering in both
  — which is the ordinary case for a comparison set, and the world E5-03's own
  respondent-count module already uses. The tests are written that way.

- **The seeder's self-check had no settled interface, and now has one.** The ticket
  fixed its behaviour and named no function, no signature and no output shape, so
  the gap was raised rather than filled by a guess. It was ruled on 2026-09-13: the
  seeder exports `the_cohort_recount(session, section_codes)`, answering counts per
  `(length_weeks, level, course_week)` — a distinct section count and a distinct
  student count, recounted from the database and never from the plan — with
  `main()` aiming it at the seeder's own codes and comparing separately against the
  two `benchmark_min_*` configuration values. The two counting tests bind to that
  name, import the script by path (`tests/fixtures/seed.py`'s `seed_module` is the
  mechanism) and hand in the codes of a cohort they planted themselves, which is
  what lets the counting be exercised without the drive. Two spellings the ruling
  does not give — the two count attributes on a returned row — stay candidate lists
  and say so.

- **Two sections of one cohort need two codes.** `BenchmarkWorld.plant_section`
  builds a section code from the cohort letter alone, so the pair it plants share
  one string. Harmless for E5-03's views, which key on length and level; wrong for a
  recount handed *codes*, where two sections behind one string would let a lookup
  resolving a code to a single row answer correctly by accident. The second section
  is given an ordinal of its own in this ticket's test module, rather than by
  changing E5-03's fixture.

- **No test invents a section code.** E4-20's refusal module could name
  `BIOL-310-R7FF` because that ticket spelled it; E5-12 spells none of its
  prior-term codes. So the refusal is read for a code of SPEC §2.2's *shape*, and
  the foreign-row test occupies whichever code the seeder itself says it is looking
  for. The pattern has canaries in both directions, because a reader that has gone
  blind looks exactly like a seeder that refuses cleanly.

- **The planning helpers got no pure-function tests**, and that is deliberate:
  naming them would settle an interface the ticket leaves open. Criterion 2 is held
  instead by two sweeps over the script's source — no system-clock reading, no
  calendar date written as a literal — each with canaries, and by the three tests
  that hold the seeded prior-term calendar to SPEC §2.2. The substitution was put to
  the owner with the other two questions and accepted on 2026-09-13: no behavioural
  planning-helper tests are to be added.

- **One record repair outside this ticket**: `tests/fixtures/comparison_sets.py`
  (E5-01) carries no fixtures and was missing from `tests/conftest.py`'s index of
  that directory. Added while adding this ticket's module, and the group's count
  corrected with it.

No MISTAKES proposals. The findings above were raised as ticket defects rather than
filed as entries, and all three were ruled on 2026-09-13: the plant's shape is
corrected in the work order, the recount's contract is settled, and the source
sweeps stand in for the planning-helper tests.

## 2026-09-13 — the prior-term calendar in `scripts/seed.py`, and one dispute

Ran the suite first, as handed over: the two controls green (the section-code
reader and the two source detectors) and the ten reds exactly as the manifest
predicts them — two missing-script sweeps, four missing-script refusals and
counting tests, and three on "the seeded calendar holds no term ending before
Fall 2026 begins".

**The calendar.** `scripts/seed.py` now seeds Spring 2026 beside Fall 2026:
eighteen weeks from Monday 12 January 2026 to Sunday 17 May, its own twenty
start-letter rows, and no section of its own. Three small changes carried it:
`seed_calendar` became a thin caller of a new parameterised `seed_term`, which
Spring's `seed_prior_calendar` calls too; `check_calendar_fits` walks both maps
and names the term in its refusal; and the summary line says what the second
term holds. The dates are the term's own — every one a Monday computed against
12 January — while the letters repeat Fall's, which is what SPEC §2.2's per-term
map permits and what the work order asks for.

The prior term ends three months before Fall 2026 begins, which matters beyond
the test that asserts it: `app.services.provisioning` puts a launched section in
the one term whose dates contain the day of the launch, so overlapping terms
would make the anchored launches of the drive depend on the hour.

**The dispute, `docs/disputes/E5-12-01.md`.** All three tests in
`test_the_seed_gives_the_prior_term_its_own_start_letter_map.py` reach `term`
from `week` and from `start_letter_map` through
`fixtures.grading.single_column_link`, and both of those tables name `term`
through ADR 0018's *composite* key alone — so the helper answers `None` on any
database at head and the three tests cannot pass against any implementation.
Measured off the models with no database involved, and recorded in the
objection. The seeding work is done and unblocked; only the confirmation is
held. The repair is three lines on the test side (`one_foreign_key_column`, the
helper `test_demo_seed_script.py` already uses against these two tables, whose
own comment records this exact trap).

## 2026-09-14 — the mock sections, the seeder, and what was verified

**The mock platform.** Four prior-term sections in `mock-lms/app/seed.py`, in a
table beside the current four: `BIOL-310-U5FF`, `BIOL-310-U6WW` and
`BIOL-310-R5FF` — twelve-week undergraduate, which is `BIOL-310-R7FF`'s own
length and level and therefore the cohort the hero section is compared against —
and `BIOL-215-E5WW`, six weeks on its own and under the section minimum. Two of
the three start on the term's first Monday and the third three weeks later, so
the course-week alignment §5.1 rests on is visible in the world rather than only
in a view test. `the_prior_term_rosters` follows `the_demo_story_roster`'s
pattern exactly: the shared instructor, then a class of the section's own; the
shared learner and the dean are in neither, and the four current-term rosters are
not touched. The ordinals (`U5`, `U6`, `R5`, `E5`) are ones nothing else in the
repository uses, so a lookup that resolved a §2.2 code alone could not confuse
one of these with a current-term section.

**The seeder.** `scripts/seed_benchmark_history.py`, on the demo story's
disciplines. Three decisions worth recording:

- **It takes no clock reading at all.** The demo story asks the clock which weeks
  have closed; this world is entirely in the past, so every course week of every
  section is written and the question never arises. That is also what makes the
  criterion-2 sweep pass without a special case.
- **The sections are resolved by `(course, term, code)`.** The foreign-row test's
  near miss is exactly this build: the planted row carries the *label*
  (`BIOL-310-U5FF`) as a section code under a course and term of its own, so the
  lookup never sees it, the run reaches the ordinary missing-section refusal, and
  the planted row is neither adopted nor touched.
- **The term is found by name, not by dates.** `PRIOR_TERM_NAME = "Spring 2026"`
  is the natural key `scripts/seed.py` upserts it under. A copy of a *name* across
  two files is the standing the demo story already has for its section label; a
  copy of the calendar would be the thing criterion 2 forbids.
- **No comments.** A benchmark figure is a workload statistic or a rating mean, so
  a prior term's comments are never shown to anybody; seeding them would mean
  verdicts and a validity recomputation for rows nothing reads. Both ratings
  therefore start at 3, which is what SPEC §3.2 requires of a response that says
  nothing on a stream.

**The happy path was driven, not assumed.** The suite covers the refusals and the
recount's currency and nothing exercises `main` end to end, so a throwaway
database was created in the development cluster, migrated, seeded, planted with
the world four anchored launches would have left (sections through
`apply_section_code`, a `user` and `enrollment` per roster member, windows through
`derive_windows_for_all_sections`), and the seeder run against it twice. First
run: 743 responses and 2229 answers across the four sections; every twelve-week
cohort week 3 sections and 54 to 59 distinct students; every six-week cohort week
1 section; exit 0 with the line saying both minimums are cleared and the thin
cohort suppresses. Second run: byte-identical counts, exit 0 — idempotent. The
database was dropped afterwards and the development one was never touched.

**Verified.** The whole unit and integration suite under `-n 4`: 3566 passed, 3
failed, and the three are the disputed calendar module and nothing else — which
also answers the question the mock-platform change raises, since several fixtures
walk *every* context the launch page offers and there are now eight. `ruff format
--check .` and `ruff check .` clean, `mypy` and `mypy mock-lms/app` clean, a full
`--collect-only` at 3569 tests, and the touched integration modules green again
with `.env` moved aside.

**Nothing proposed for `docs/MISTAKES.md`.** Nothing here went wrong in a way an
entry would have prevented; the one finding is the dispute above.

## 2026-09-14 — E5-12-01 upheld, and the three-line repair on the test side

The objection is right and the defect is mine. All three calendar tests reached
`term` from `week` and `start_letter_map` with `fixtures.grading.single_column_link`,
which by construction answers only for a *single-column* foreign key; ADR 0018
gives both tables one composite key, `(term_id, term_length_weeks) → term (id,
length_weeks)`, so the helper answers `None` on every database at head. Three
correct assertions were failing on the route rather than on the rows they are
about, and no seeding work could have changed that.

Repaired in `tests/integration/test_the_seed_gives_the_prior_term_its_own_start_letter_map.py`
and nowhere else:

- the `fixtures.grading` import is gone;
- a local `term_link_column(tables, name)` follows the link's **primary-key**
  column — the shape `one_foreign_key_column` uses in `test_demo_seed_script.py`
  against these same two tables, whose own comment records this trap — and fails
  loudly, naming the table's columns, if there is not exactly one such column;
- `map_rows_for` and the week-rows test call it instead of the single-column
  helper. `fixtures/grading.py` is untouched: other tests pin its strictness about
  composite keys, and widening it to serve this module would move a rule they
  depend on.

**No assertion changed.** The three tests still say: the prior term ends before
Fall 2026 begins and carries week rows 1..N; its map dates lie inside its own term
and share none with Fall's; and every map row starts on a Monday a whole number of
weeks into its term, runs one of SPEC §2.2's lengths and ends inside it. The
module docstring now records the composite-link route and points at the dispute,
so the next reader meets the trap before the helper.

**What this cost, as a lesson**: a helper was reused for the shape of its question
rather than for the answer it can give. `single_column_link` is named for exactly
what it cannot do here, and one look at ADR 0018 — which this module cites twice
for other reasons — would have said so before the tests were written.

## 2026-09-14 — the three battery survivors, and the world that kills them

All three survived for one reason, and it is a single sentence: **every test in
this suite stopped at a refusal, so the seeder's write path was never executed by
anything.** The refusals are where a seeder's risk usually is; they are not where
all of it is, and a suite that only ever meets a program's first "no" cannot see
what its "yes" does.

The repair is a world rather than an assertion.
`plant_a_launched_prior_term_world` (in `tests/fixtures/benchmark_history.py`)
plants, row by row, what four anchored staff launches and a roster sync would have
left: the sections under the courses their codes name, in the seeded prior term,
with a `survey_window` for every course week and a twenty-student roster enrolled
from the section's first day. It plants no `response` and no `answer` — the
seeder's own work is all still to do, so `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 48's rule is
untouched: the test stands in for the platform here, not for the seeder.

**The world is built out of the seeder's own refusals.** Run it; plant whatever it
says is missing; run it again, up to a bounded number of rounds. No test names a
section code, counts the sections, or depends on whether a refusal lists one or all
of them. A run that refuses while naming nothing new is reported as what it is —
the world is short of something the builder does not plant, with the whole output
attached — because that sentence is the next reader's only clue.

What the three new or strengthened tests do:

- **The foreign section** (battery row 3) is now planted *complete but in Fall
  2026*: a window for every course week and a roster of its own, so the term is the
  only thing wrong with it. The first version planted a bare `section` row, and a
  code-alone adopter found it and then stopped on its own next check — no windows —
  which satisfied every assertion. A world an adopter cannot get past cannot tell
  an adopter from a correct build.
- **The foreign response** (row 4) is planted at a key the seeder is *known* to
  write, learned by letting it write the world once and then clearing its rows.
  Guessing a key it might not choose would have been a test that could pass by
  missing.
- **The week predicate** (row 12) is caught by counting keys rather than rows: more
  `(student, section, week)` keys than `(student, section)` pairs. A lookup that has
  lost its week updates the week-1 row when week 2 is written, and a term of weekly
  rows becomes one row per student — invisible to every other test here. Its
  companion finally makes criterion 3's run-twice proof on the write path, row by
  row including `created_at`, which is the only comparison that catches a
  delete-and-rewrite.

**The first run of these tests refused over a world holding all four sections**,
and the cause was in the builder rather than in the seeder: it passed the term and
the prefix to the seeding walker as *chain* entries and let it fill the rest, so
the planted sections hung on rows the walker was free to invent. A section attached
to anything but the seed's own prior-term row is one the seeder's
`(prefix code, course number, term, section code)` lookup cannot find, and the
symptom is a refusal naming sections that are visibly in the database. Every link
is now written out column by column by `link_values`, which follows the foreign
keys and so fills a composite link in full (ADR 0018 again); the walker's chain is
not used in the planting at all. `describe_the_sections` prints all four lookup
keys, plus the window and enrollment counts, into that failure message — so a
refusal of this shape now says which key is wrong instead of leaving it to be
guessed, and says whether the answer is a defect in the builder or a row it does
not plant.

**The follow-the-keys helper then failed on the one table that names a row without
a key of its own.** `survey_window` carries `term_id` and holds no foreign key into
`term`: its only constraint into the calendar is the composite `(week_id, term_id)`
into `week` — E2-05's "window's term rule", which makes a window's term its week's
term and enforces it through the week rather than beside it. Asking for a key that
is not there raised inside a module-scoped fixture, so one wrong assumption came
back as two setup ERRORs and took the green foreign-section test down with it.
`link_values` now follows a foreign key where there is one and fills a
`<table>_<primary key>` column from the row in hand where there is not; the window's
values are ordered so the week's constrained pair wins if the two ever disagree.
The shape of the mistake is `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 35's: a helper that enumerates
one mechanism for a relationship will eventually meet the design that uses another.

That run also exposed a second defect in the builder, and it is the reason one
wrong assumption cost two ERRORs instead of two named failures: the builder caught
`Exception`, and `pytest.fail` raises `Failed`, which derives from `BaseException`.
So every guard inside `plant_one_launch` escaped the builder and landed at setup —
`docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 44 from the one direction a returning builder still had
open. It now catches `BaseException`, re-raising `KeyboardInterrupt` and
`SystemExit`, and puts the guard's own sentence at the front of `problem`.

**The real mismatch was the currency of the section code, and a dump of the
database is what found it.** `section.lms_section_code` stores SPEC §2.2's **bare**
code — every seeded section in this repository holds `U1WW`, `R1FF`, `21WW` — while
the builder planted the whole label, `BIOL-310-U5FF`. The seeder matches
`Section.lms_section_code == "U5FF"`, so its refusal was right and the planted world
was unreachable. The same wrong currency had already made an assertion vacuous: the
unlaunched-world test's "the section the refusal named is not in this database" ran
a query that could never match whatever it was handed, so it was true of every
possible world — `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 3, in the line written to prevent exactly
that — and the foreign-section test's "collision" was not a collision at all, which
is why it passed while proving nothing about adoption. A green test was hiding the
same defect the reds were shouting about.

The two currencies are now named where they are read, converted by one helper
(`bare_code`), and every caller says which it holds: **labels** for refusal text
and for the keys `world.launches` uses, **bare codes** for `lms_section_code`.
`sections_coded` takes a label, looks the code up, and takes an optional `term` —
a code is per-term data, so an unnarrowed lookup answers about another term's
section of the same name. `PlantedLaunch` carries both forms, and the
foreign-section test now asserts that exactly one section carries the planted code,
which is the assertion that says its collision is real.

**And the last gap: a roster is a set of subjects, not a count of enrollments.**
The seeder does not ask how many students are enrolled; it asks for the students
whose `user.lms_user_id` begins with the mock platform's generated prefix for that
placement — `mock-lms-user-biol-310-u5ff-student-`, which its refusal quotes in
full. Twenty enrollments whose users carried walker-invented subjects were twenty
rows and no roster, and the refusal was right again. `plant_one_launch` now plants
each student under that subject, built from the label the way the platform builds
it and transcribed from the refusal rather than imported from the seeder
(`docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 19); the two-digit tail is this suite's own, since the
seeder matches on the prefix and needs only that the subjects differ.

**And the last red was the test demanding a refusal where adoption is correct.**
This seeder's writes are deterministic, so a response whose whole natural key —
`(student, section, week)` — is in its plan is **its own row by definition**,
whoever put it there, and normalising it on a later run is exactly what criterion 3
asks for. The foreign-response test had learned a key the seeder *had written* and
planted a copy at it, which is that row and not a stranger's: the guard was right
and the world was wrong. Foreign means a `(student, week)` pair inside a section
the seeder fills that its plan cannot account for. The test now plants a transfer
student — enrolled in a planned section, with a subject outside the roster prefix
the seeder reads — answering in a week it wrote. That row belongs to somebody, no
plan describes it, and the refusal exists for it.

The nuance is worth keeping beside `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 31: **"a row the loader
did not write" is not the same question as "a row the loader's own rules do not
produce"**, and for a deterministic loader it is the second that matters. A test
written to the first wording asks for a refusal on the loader's own output.

Five rounds, five wrong assumptions about the same world: the ancestor chain, the
window's term link, the section code's currency, the roster's, and what foreign
means to a deterministic writer. The first four were claims about rows this builder
could have read back and checked rather than assumed, and the round that moved
fastest was the one where the database was dumped — **when the thing under test
refuses a world a test planted, read the world back before changing the test.** The
fifth was a different mistake and a worse one: a claim about the *thing under test's
own rules*, made without reading them, in a test whose whole subject is those
rules.

Two housekeeping moves came with it: the calendar readers (`prior_term`,
`map_rows_for`, `term_link_column` and the rest) moved from the calendar module
into the shared fixture module, because the world builder asks the same questions
and `term_link_column` is a worked-around quirk that must not be worked around
twice (entry 13); and `tests/conftest.py`'s index entry says what that module now
holds.

## 2026-09-14 — a note added by E5-SEED, nothing above edited

The account above is left exactly as it was written. One thing it records needs a
correction beside it: the throwaway-database drive reported "exit 0 with the line
