# E5-04 — attempts

## 2026-09-13 — the red tests, and two things the work order could not settle

Wrote the failing tests for all eight acceptance criteria, the definer-reach
test the deferred file asks for, the rewrite of the comparison-set grant module
and the two `RUNTIME_BASE_TABLE_PRIVILEGES` entries. What the writing turned up:

- **The definer equality cannot be a `(relation, column)` equality yet, and the
  reason is a missing record rather than a missing test.** The work order asks
  for `BENCHMARK_DEFINER_PRIVILEGES` "transcribed from
  `backend/app/views_sql/benchmark_definer_v001.sql`'s six GRANT lines". A
  constant transcribed from the SQL it polices asserts that the SQL equals
  itself, which is `docs/MISTAKES.md` entry 19 and which
  `test_each_definer_holds_exactly_the_column_privileges_its_job_needs` refuses
  in its own docstring: "the expected sets are derived from the records' own
  sentences rather than copied from the migration". The records that could
  supply the columns do not — ADR 0165, E5-03's attempts log and
  `docs/tickets/e5/deferred.md` all stop at the six *relations*. So the test
  asserts every grain the records do settle (the six relations as an equality in
  both directions, `SELECT` and no other verb, no column of `user`,
  `user_identity` or `person`, and nothing held at the grain of a whole
  relation, which ADR 0165 rejects by name) and says in its own docstring that
  the column equality is owed. **The answer is a record rather than a narrowed
  deferral.** ADR 0165 is the definer's own record and gains a sentence naming
  the exact `(relation, column)` `SELECT` pairs — a claim a reviewer can weigh in
  this pull request, with the SQL never entering a test. The equality then
  extends to column grain derived from that sentence, in the sibling test's
  shape, and the deferred entry closes in full. The test as written is the red
  run's version; the column grain lands on top of it in this same ticket.
- **The work order and the wave brief disagree about a migration.** The work
  order has this ticket ship
  `backend/app/views_sql/comparison_set_grants_v001.sql` and "a new migration off
  chain head `a7c3e9d21f84` executing it"; the common brief says "only E5-12 may
  add one … E5-02, E5-04, E5-08 must add NO migration". The tests are written to
  the work order, because the ticket's own scope names the grant ("the first read
  of E5-01's tables lands here") and a grants file with no revision to run it
  grants nothing. Both the rewritten `SELECT` test and the two
  `RUNTIME_BASE_TABLE_PRIVILEGES` entries stay red until that revision exists,
  so this is a decision the ticket cannot be finished without. **Settled the
  same day: the work order governs.** This ticket ships the grants file and a
  grants-only revision off `a7c3e9d21f84`, and the wave brief's line saying
  otherwise was wrong and has been corrected there. The two tests stand as
  written.

Worlds, briefly: every population is planted at the configured minimums read

## 2026-09-13 — the service, and why it does not import `views_sql/queries.py`

The red run reproduced first: the four planter controls, the provenance-check
control, the six write refusals and the definer relation-grain test are green on
the tree as it stands, and 21 assertions are red — the eighteen service tests
(five suppression, six population, seven provenance parameters), the two
`SELECT` grant parameters and the `RUNTIME_BASE_TABLE_PRIVILEGES` equality. That
is the manifest's twenty rows counted by parameter rather than by row.

`backend/app/services/benchmarks.py` turned all eighteen service tests green on
its first run. What it took to write:

- **The service cannot import `app.views_sql.queries`, and E5-03's comment on
  that module says the opposite.** That comment reads "Written here rather than
  in the service that will call them so that E5-04 has no reason to spell a
  statement of its own — which is the state
  `test_the_org_views_are_read_only_through_the_grant.py` exists to prevent".
  The sweep's import half does the reverse: it excuses exactly one importer of
  that module, `backend/app/services/authz.py`, and reds every other module
  under `backend/app/` that imports it, by name or relatively. So importing the
  two typed wrappers would have turned a green gate red, and moving them out of
  `queries.py` is not open either — E5-03's own suite reads them there by name.
  The two statements are therefore spelled in `benchmarks.py`, which is the
  route `backend/app/api/dev.py` already takes for `section_enrollment_count`
  and says so in its own comment. Neither set function is a relation that sweep
  polices — the inventory is parsed from `CREATE VIEW` bodies and both are
  functions — so no exemption is involved and the sweep is green. The
  duplication of two statements is real and is recorded in
  `docs/tickets/e5/deferred.md`; the false sentence in `queries.py` is corrected
  in this pull request's records commit.
- **Everything else is read through SQLAlchemy Core**, because `section`,
  `course`, `lead_faculty_course` and the four cohort views *are* in that
  sweep's policed inventory and a statement naming one in SQL text would be an
  offender. `app.services.reporting` reads the report views the same way.
- **The counts a rating figure is suppressed against come from the workload row
  for the same week.** `benchmark_set_rating_week` answers `rating_count`, which
  counts ratings rather than people, so neither minimum can be compared against
  it; `benchmark_set_week` is where `section_count` and the distinct count of
  people live. Both functions are called for a trend.
- **The empty section list is answered without a round trip.** An empty Python
  list gives the driver no element type to infer for the `uuid[]` argument, and
  the error that produces would read as a defect in the function — which is the
  reason the benchmark world fixture gives for keeping the empty call out of its
  own helper. No sections means no rows, which is a suppressed figure at every
  call site.
- **A section with no lead, and a section id that names nothing, resolve to no
  sections rather than raising.** No criterion covers either; both withhold a
  figure rather than failing a report on an input the caller read out of the
  same database, and both are stated in the resolvers' docstrings.
- **`named_set_term_axis` answers the cohort the set declares rather than its
  member courses.** The term-axis views are keyed by `(length, level, term,
  start date)` and hold no course, so there is no way to narrow one to a
  membership list. Stated in the function's docstring and in ADR 0166 rather
  than left for E5-06 to discover.

## 2026-09-13 — the grants, the migration, and the records

The grant is `backend/app/views_sql/comparison_set_grants_v001.sql`, in the
shape `weekly_summary_grants_v001.sql` models, and revision `d9e5b13c7a42`
executes it off `a7c3e9d21f84` — read off `ScriptDirectory.walk_revisions()`,
which reported one head and 44 revisions before the cut and `d9e5b13c7a42` as
the only head after it. `alembic upgrade head` and `alembic check` both pass on
the development database, and the revision was walked down and back up: after
`downgrade -1` `has_table_privilege('pulse_app', …, 'SELECT')` is false on both
tables and true again after `upgrade head`, so the named `REVOKE` is the real
mirror of the `GRANT` rather than a statement nobody ran.

The ADR 0165 amendment was **checked before it was written**, twice and in both
directions: the eighteen `(relation, column)` pairs parsed out of
`benchmark_definer_v001.sql`'s `GRANT` lines and the eighteen read out of
`pg_attribute`'s column ACLs on the migrated database are the same set, the only
verb is `SELECT`, and there is no table-wide grant to the owner. That matters
because the sentence is what the column-grain equality will be derived from: a
transcription nobody checked would have moved the entry 19 problem from the test
into the record.

Both E5-04-owned entries in `docs/tickets/e5/deferred.md` are answered and
closed in place rather than deleted, and one new entry is opened — the two
statements `queries.py` and `benchmarks.py` both hold, owned by E5-14.
