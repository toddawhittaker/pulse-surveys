# E5.1-02 — The roster decides who teaches and who answers

**ID:** E5.1-02
**Branch:** `e5.1/roster-grants-and-respondents`
**Depends on:** none
**Lane:** heavy ⚠ (SPEC §14.3 puts the grant ending under line-by-line human
review)
**Size:** M–L
**Security-relevant:** the migration adds a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that
writes `role_assignment`, an identity table, and the ticket edits
`backend/app/lti/`. A defect here leaves a removed instructor reading their old
section, or counts staff as students.

## Context

Three defects in the roster sync:

- **The teaching grant is never ended.** `_ingest` only ever adds it
  (`backend/app/services/roster_sync.py:1450-1451`, and
  `_record_the_teaching_instructor` at `:1643`). The predicates in
  `backend/app/services/authz.py:439-464` assume a removed instructor's grant
  row is gone. This ticket does not edit those predicates; it makes their
  assumption true.
- **Staff and LMS test users count as respondents.** An enrollment is written
  for every roster member (`roster_sync.py:1449`), and a launch with no
  assignment but with an enrollment lands as STUDENT
  (`authz.py:1746-1752`).
- **The roster's token grant has no time bound.** `roster_sync.py:442` builds
  an unbounded `requests.Session`, while `backend/app/lti/ags.py:648` bounds
  its own. This closes the carried entry "the roster sync's unbounded
  token-acquisition dial". Rehoming the shared transport stays carried.

Also: the docstring at `backend/app/lti/__init__.py:20-23` says `pylti1p3` is
not used for launch verification. It is.

Read first: SPEC §2.1, §4.1, §7.3, and §14.3's E9 entry (end-dating is E9's);
the migration that created `record_teaching_instructor`
(`backend/migrations/versions/20260827_e2c94b6a1f70_the_roster_sync_writes_windows_and_calls.py`);
`docs/MISTAKES.md` entries 41 and 46.

## Done when

1. **A removed instructor loses the section.** A complete walk drops a member,
   or drops that member's `membership#Instructor` role. Their next
   `GET /instructor/sections` omits the section, and `GET …/report/{week}`
   answers the same 404 body as a section that does not exist. The test is
   invariant-marked and asserts the refusal itself, not an empty list.
2. **A partial walk ends nothing.** A truncated walk (`complete=False`) ends
   no grant.
3. **The ending goes through a guarded definer.** The grant is ended through a
   `SECURITY DEFINER` function that is a sibling of
   `record_teaching_instructor`. The runtime role still holds no `DELETE` on
   `role_assignment`, and a grant query asserts that. The function refuses any
   assignment that is not section-scoped `INSTRUCTOR`. Test it through the
   connection production uses (entry 46).
   The same function writes a row to a new append-only table,
   `ended_teaching_grant`, in the same transaction. The row names the ended
   assignment, the person, the section, the role, the day it ended, and the
   roster walk that ended it. The runtime role can insert into that table only
   through the function; it holds no `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on it. A test through
   the production connection shows that a grant cannot be ended without its
   row, and that the row survives the deletion. `audit_log` is not touched: it
   holds identity reveals only, needs an acting person, and is what E10's Care
   reader reads.
4. **Teaching members and test users hold no student enrollment.** A roster
   member carrying Instructor, or `…/lti/system/person#TestUser`, holds no open
   enrollment after a walk, and an existing open one is closed. Such a
   person's launch does not land as STUDENT for that section, and
   `POST /student/submissions` refuses them.
5. **The token grant has a bound.** The roster's token grant runs under a
   bound. A token endpoint that accepts the connection and then stalls fails
   that section within the bound. Time it against a stalling stub (entry 41).
6. **The records match.** `lti/__init__.py` says that `pylti1p3` validates
   launches. ADR 0183 records two things: the grant is deleted rather than
   end-dated (end-dating is E9's, per SPEC §14.3), and enrollment means
   "student".

## Known traps

- **The privilege belongs to the role that runs the code.** Prove the definer
  and the missing `DELETE` against the production connection, not the
  migration owner (entry 46).
- **A refusal test needs a readable control.** Criterion 1 shows the section
  readable before the walk in the same world.

## Out of scope

- End-dating assignments. That is E9's.
- Moving the transport into `lti/`, or `roster_sync` into `lti/nrps.py`. The
  carried entry keeps its owner.
