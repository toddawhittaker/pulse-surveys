## 2026-09-08 — D1, the move (commit 255fcd4) — worked, with one departure

`git mv` of the four `instructorReport*Copy.ts` files into
`frontend/src/copy/`, imports rewritten in ten components and two routes, and
the three stale header paragraphs replaced (plus the same claim in
`lib/landings.ts`).

**The move alone did not parse.** `instructorReportStatCopy.ts` carries
`formatStatistic` and `formatRate` below its object literal, and both name a
copy key — `copy('instructor_report_stats.absent_figure')`. The inventory
parser refuses any quotation mark outside the literal, in either direction, so
the first run after the move went from 7 reds to 14: `collect_frontend_copy`
raised `CopyParseError` at that line and every rule reading the frontend half
failed with it.

Considered and rejected: disputing the parser rule (it is deliberate E2
machinery with its own refusal controls, and "a sentence returned by a helper
below the literal" is one of them — the rule reads a quotation mark, not a
meaning, and that bluntness is the point); a defaulted parameter or a
caller-supplied fallback (both change an exported signature the frontend tests
hold); reaching the entry without naming its key (nothing in TypeScript does
that without being clever). Taken: the two formatters moved to a new
`components/instructorReportFigures.ts` and import `copy`/`fillCopy` from the
copy module. Rounding a percent is presentation, not words, so this is where
they belonged. It is a departure from D1's "a move, not a rewrite" and it
crosses a module boundary, so it is called out rather than buried.

After the move: 4 reds left of the original 7 — the two gradebook rows (D4),
the credit note (D9), and the sweep, whose findings are down from 205 in 16
files to the 2 in `UnknownAddress.tsx` that D6 fixes. Frontend: typecheck and
eslint clean; vitest 111 passed, 1 failed —
`PulseTrendChart.test.tsx` reads `instructorReportTrendCopy.ts` by its old path
(`COMPONENT_SOURCES`, line 488) and holds the old import spelling in `ALLOWED`
(line 596). Both are the orchestrator's to repair.
