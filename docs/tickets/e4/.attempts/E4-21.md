## 1. The histogram's two boxes — worked, and disproved the audit's reason

`design/RatingHistogram.dc.html:14-24` draws a 96px row of counts over bars and
a separate tick row under it; the implementation drew one 96px column per bucket
holding all three, with a `border-top` on every tick rather than one rule on the
row. Restoring the two boxes was mechanical. Splitting the mean line was not
quite: the mockup sets the figure in full spruce inside a spruce-60 line, a
colour cannot reach half a text node, so `instructor_report_stats.distribution_mean`
(`mean {mean}`) became `distribution_mean_label` (`mean`) with the figure in its
own element beside it. The label stays governed copy; the figure is a number.

**What the single column actually cost, measured.** The ticket says to verify
the overflow on screen before and after, the audit having read it off the CSS
and called it probable. It is not what happens. Both layouts were rendered in
Chromium through Vite — the real component, the real stylesheet, six
distributions including a three-digit count and an empty week — and in neither
does any count leave the chart region or reach the mean line. The bar is a flex
item, so flexbox shrank it instead: the tallest bar drew at **47px of the 72px**
`barHeight` asks for, so every distribution was drawn at about two thirds of the
prototype's scale. After the change the tallest bar measures 72px and the counts
still sit inside the box. The fix is the same fix; its reason is different, and
`474721f` corrects every comment that had repeated the prediction as fact.

The harness (a throwaway `harness.tsx`/`harness.html` under `frontend/`, a
`vite` dev server on 5199, and a Playwright script) was deleted after the
measurement; `git status` was checked clean.

## 2. The trend axis over the whole term — worked

`lengthWeeks` is a required prop on `PulseTrendChart` and on `TrendPair`, not an
optional one: a default of `points.length` is the elapsed-weeks axis this ticket
removes, arriving silently wherever a caller forgot it. Two consequences worth
naming. A point is placed by its own `courseWeek` rather than by its index,
because published weeks have holes — the page fixture's own trend steps 2, 4 —
and an index would draw week 4 where week 3 belongs. And a week the report has
not reached gets a course-week tick with nothing under it: SPEC §2.2 forbids
deriving a term week the server has not sent, which is exactly what the mockup's
`termOffset` does.

Mutation-checked both ways. `axisWeeks` returning `points.length` reds five
tests across three files; placing points by index reds the holes test alone, and
the mutation was confirmed present in the file before the run was believed.

## 3. `TERM_LENGTH` and `termWeeks` — caught by a standing gate

`tests/unit/test_no_frontend_source_derives_how_long_a_section_runs.py` refuses
an identifier under `frontend/src/` naming the term's own calendar. The axis work
had written a test constant `TERM_LENGTH` and a local `termWeeks`, and the guard
is right: both are about the *section* — how many weeks it runs for, and which
term week each of its course weeks fell in. Renamed to `SECTION_WEEKS` and
`byCourseWeek` (`25a735c`). Nothing about the guard was touched.

## 4. The small-N notice, the card and the header row — worked

The notice renders once, under both comment groups, where
`design/InstructorMondayReport.dc.html:69-73` puts it: the suppression is a fact
about the week, and inside the first group it read as a statement about that
group's comments. `CommentGroup`'s `smallN: {threshold, withNotice}` became a
required `suppressed: boolean` — required because a caller who forgot it would
be showing a below-threshold week's raw comments. Its copy regains the leading
count and the "AI summary above" phrasing, so the notice now renders three
numbers; `SmallNNotice.test.tsx`'s digit sweep asserts exactly those three and
says why none of them is a count of what was withheld (§5.2).

The card, the 760px column, the arrows in the eyebrow row at 28px, the subline
and the divider spacing are all stylesheet and placement. jsdom applies no CSS,
so `reportMockupFidelity.test.ts` reads the rules as text — the shape
`reportContrastTokens.test.ts` already uses for the boundary round's contrast
corrections. Two of its pins were mutation-checked (the column back at 720px,
the comment card back to the display face); both went red.

## 5. The eyebrow's comma-plus-middot — fixed on the report only

The artifact is two separators between the first two segments: the copy entry
`student_survey.course_week_eyebrow` ends in a comma — pinned byte-for-byte by
`tests/unit/test_the_student_surveys_ruled_copy_is_in_the_governed_inventory.py`
under the ruling of 2026-09-07 — and `styles.css` prepends `· ` to every quiet
segment. So the copy cannot change, and the fix is in the stylesheet: the
segment that follows the course-week half loses the middot and keeps the comma,
and anything after it keeps the middot, which is the separator the same ruling
puts before the closing instant.

**Scoped to `.pulse-report`, on the work order's instruction that the student
survey's eyebrow render unchanged.** The student survey shows the same artifact
from the same copy and the same rule, and the ruled rendering — `COURSE WK 04 /
12, TERM WK 07 · closes Sun 11:59 PM` — says it should not. One selector without
the `.pulse-report` prefix would fix both surfaces; that is one line and the
owner's call, and it is left unmade here rather than taken quietly.
